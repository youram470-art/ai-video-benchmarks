Reverse video search: three clips, three outcomes

What happened when I tried to trace three short clips back to their source, and which frame strategy worked.

I picked three clips with unknown origins and tried to trace them. One was easy, one needed a mirrored frame, one never resolved.

The middle frame carried the most context in two of three cases. The first second is usually a fade or a title, and the last second is often a logo animation, so neither matches anything. If you only search one frame, search the middle.

The clip that needed an upscale was 640 pixels wide and heavily compressed. Doubling it and stripping the ringing before searching flipped a miss into a hit, which suggests the engines are matching texture as much as content. The loop I use for this is written up at reverse video search.

Mirroring solved a re-upload that had been flipped to dodge detection. If a frame returns nothing but looks familiar, flip it horizontally and try again; it costs ten seconds.

The unresolved clip had burned-in subtitles in a language I could not identify, and the crop removed every edge cue. Without a watermark, an edge, or readable text, there is nothing to match on, and no amount of retrying fixes a frame that has been stripped of context.
