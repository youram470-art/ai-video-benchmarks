Seedance 2.5: the ten-second ceiling in practice

Where Seedance 2.5 holds up past eight seconds, and which camera moves break first.

Ten seconds is the advertised ceiling. In practice the useful ceiling depends on the move, so I timed where each clip starts to drift.

shot typeattemptskeptnote
locked off, no motion10.0s9.6s
slow push in10.0s7.2s
orbit around subject10.0s5.8s
handheld follow10.0s4.4s

Locked-off shots hold almost the full duration. Anything that changes the camera position starts losing the subject much earlier, and a handheld follow is gone before the halfway mark. The number that matters is not the ceiling but the last frame you can cut on.

Camera control is the reason to use this model, and it is also the reason to plan shorter shots. A push in that reads well at six seconds is worth more than a ten-second clip you trim to five, because you skip the frames where the model starts improvising.

Audio drifts before video does in my tests, by around two frames over eight seconds. For anything with a spoken line I mute the generated track and use the picture only. The workflow notes at Seedance 2.5 describe the same behaviour on longer clips.

Two settings changed results more than any prompt wording: an explicit start frame, and a negative prompt naming the artefacts I kept seeing (extra fingers, floating objects, text). Neither is glamorous, both are repeatable.

Measured on consumer hardware in November 2026. No sponsored placement; every number comes from a logged run.
