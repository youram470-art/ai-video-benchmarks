Cutting a 90-second fan film from 22 clips

A production log for a 90-second fan film made with AI video: beat sheet, shot budget, and the three shots that saved it.

Ninety seconds, twenty-two clips, one weekend. Three shots did most of the work, and none of them were the ones I planned first.

The establishing shot was the least important clip in the film. I regenerated it four times trying to get a skyline right, then replaced it with a still and a slow push, which took four minutes and looked better than any of the generations.

The shots that carried the film were a rear-view mirror, a hand on a door handle, and a pair of shoes walking. All three are close, all three hide faces, and all three cut against anything. If you are planning a fan film, budget half your shots as details like these; the pattern is documented at GTA 6 AI video.

Cutting to a scratch track was the difference between a slide show and a scene. Twenty-two clips at eight seconds is nearly three minutes of material, so half of it had to go, and the track decided which half.

One rule kept the project out of trouble: no real logos, no real people, and a disclaimer in the description. Fan work survives on being obviously fan work, and nothing in the edit pretends otherwise.
