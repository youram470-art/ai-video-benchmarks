H3 video: 12 prompts, 4 usable clips

A logged test of twelve H3 prompts across talking head, walk and pan shots, with what survived editing.

I ran twelve prompts through H3 in one sitting and kept four clips. This is the log, including the ones I would not show anyone.

The pattern was consistent: static shots survive, anything with translation does not. If the subject crosses the frame, the model spends its budget on the crossing and stops tracking the details you actually care about.

The two talking-head clips that worked shared a prompt shape: subject description first, then a fixed camera statement, then a lighting note, and nothing else. Adding a second sentence about the room cut my keep rate roughly in half, which matches what I saw when writing up H3 video earlier.

I also stopped asking for gestures. A hand raise or a head turn moves the whole upper body and the model re-invents the shirt every time. A still subject with a fixed camera is boring to watch for eight seconds, but it cuts cleanly against a wide, and that is what a scene needs.

Cost per usable second came out at roughly three times the sticker price once retries were counted. That number, not the demo reel, is what decided whether I kept using it for this project.

Measured on consumer hardware in November 2026. No sponsored placement; every number comes from a logged run.
