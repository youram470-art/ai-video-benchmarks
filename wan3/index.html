Wan 3 batch: what 60 clips actually cost

Credit and time accounting for a 60-clip Wan 3 batch, including retries and the shots that never worked.

Sixty clips, four evenings, one spreadsheet. This is what the batch cost and which shot types ate the budget.

shot typeattemptskeptnote
wide establishing123.1 credits/clip
medium dialogue184.4 credits/clip
detail insert202.2 credits/clip
crowd or hands107.8 credits/clip

Crowds and hands cost more than twice what a detail insert cost, because they needed more attempts. If I had priced the batch by clip count instead of by shot type, the last evening would have run out of credits with the hard shots still queued.

The cheap shots are the ones worth generating in bulk. Inserts, hands on objects, screens, doors: they are short, they cut well, and they hide transitions. A scene built from a wide, two mediums and three inserts feels more complete than the same runtime of mediums alone.

Time was the other surprise. Rendering sixty clips took less wall-clock time than reviewing them, because review means watching each clip twice and deciding. The budget worksheet at Wan 3 now includes a review column for that reason.

I capped retries at three per shot. On the fourth attempt I changed the shot instead of the prompt, which is the single rule that kept the batch from doubling in cost.
