Grok images: 40 generations, 11 keepers

A batch test of forty Grok image prompts, sorted by what failed: hands, text, lighting and composition.

Forty prompts, eleven images I would publish. The failures clustered into four kinds, and three of them are preventable before you generate.

shot typeattemptskeptnote
single subject, plain bg127
two subjects interacting101
sign or label in frame80
product on surface103

Two subjects is where it falls apart. The model gives each subject a plausible face and then loses track of which body belongs to whom, usually at the hands or the shoulder line. Text in frame never worked: eight attempts, zero readable results, which is a strong enough signal to stop trying.

What did work was treating the prompt like a shot description rather than a wish. Subject, framing, light direction, surface. Four clauses, no adjectives about mood. The QA pass I wrote for Grok image generator is essentially this list turned into checks.

Lighting consistency is worth checking at 200% before you publish. A highlight on the left of the face and a shadow on the left of the object underneath is the tell that survives downscaling, and it is easier to regenerate than to paint out.

Composition was the pleasant surprise: the model respects 'wide shot' and 'top-down' more reliably than it respects 'cinematic'. Plain photographic vocabulary beats style words, at least in this batch.
